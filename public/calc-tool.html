DTI Guideline: Lenders qualify on gross income vs. full PITI only — no credit for tax savings. Front-end ≤28% preferred; ≤36% back-end (all debts); up to 43–45% with strong compensating factors.
Co-Borrower Note (per Fannie Mae B3-3.4-12):
Anna’s IHSS income can only count toward DTI if she is a co-borrower on the loan. Non-borrower income cannot offset DTI under standard Fannie Mae rules. Adding Anna as co-borrower means: credit check, all her debts added to back-end DTI, legal obligation on the note, and any derogatory credit affects the analysis.

Is it worth it? The DTI improvement is modest (typically 1.5–2.0 points). IHSS income is hourly/variable and requires documented history. The real test is back-end DTI — if all debts are comfortably under 43–45%, Anna’s income may not change the lender’s decision.

Effective DTI is a planning tool only. Lenders do not reduce PITI by estimated tax savings when calculating qualifying DTI. The effective cost figures above are for your personal cash flow analysis — not for lender qualification.
